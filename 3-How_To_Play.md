# How to Play 1-Key-Finesse
If you've downloaded Tampermonkey and Justin1L8's 1KF script on Jstris, or if you're on any 1KF client, you're good to go!\
Otherwise, first head over to [this page](https://github.com/sillyshark321/1-Key-Finesse/blob/main/2-Getting_Started.md)

## Hardware needed:
Literally any computer keyboard, preferably with more than 40 keys\
(Numbers 1-0, all letters A-Z, punctuation ; , . /)

## Recommended prerequisites
- Know how to touch type, or at least know how to do it
  - Touch typing allows you to type without having to look down and reference the letters on your keycaps - this is done by **resting your fingers** on `ASDF JKL;`
- Know basic stacking principles in guideline Tetris / falling block stacking games
- Get your 40L Sprint time under 1 minute with guideline controls
- **Have lots and lots and lots of patience!**

## It’s so confusing! How does it work?
Although 1KF control schemes use 41 keys minimum, the controls are quite easy to understand. Typically, you’ll use these keys:

![Keyboard 1](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sillyshark321/1-Key-Finesse/main/media/key1.png)

Starting off horizontally - the different keyboard rows correspond to different piece orientations.

- Number row: 180° rotation
- Top row (`QWER`...): 90° Counter-clockwise rotation
- Home row (`ASDF`...): Spawn / No rotation
- Bottom row (`ZXCV`...): 90° Clockwise rotation

Now looking vertically, the keyboard columns dictate the columnar position of your pieces. Keyboard columns are `1QAZ`, `2WSX`, etc. all the way to `0P;/`.

Here’s a diagram showing how the pieces would move in the board corresponding to each row and column:

![Keyboard 2](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sillyshark321/1-Key-Finesse/main/media/key2.png)

Every key in 1KF can also be expressed with multiple guideline controls in the form of instantaneous macros. This diagram demonstrates the same 1KF controls (hard drop is always pressed after):

![Keyboard 3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sillyshark321/1-Key-Finesse/main/media/key3.png)

There are other columnar alignments featured in older clients, but this one proves to be the most effective.

## 3-Step Process ⭐

To handle your piece placements with 1KF controls properly, you’ll need to follow the 3-step process:

### [1] Find which wall is closer
Self-explanatory.\
If your piece placement is closer to the left wall, use your left hand. Otherwise, use your right hand.

### [2] Use the correct orientation
Generally, your fingers should be resting on the home row (`ASDF JKL;`) for no rotation.\
If your piece placement is clockwise, move your fingers down to the bottom row (`ZXCV M,./`).\
If it’s counterclockwise, move up to the top row (`QWER UIOP`).\
If it’s upside-down, move to the number row (`1234 7890`).

### [3] Pick the right column
This is a very crucial step - do not rush this!\
If you moved your fingers strictly on the keyboard columns:

- Your pinky finger placements are touching the wall.
- Your ring finger placements are 1 space away from the wall.
- Your middle finger placements are 2 spaces away from the wall.
- Your index finger placements are either 3 or 4 spaces away from the wall, depending on if you move laterally to the inner keyboard columns.

As an exercise, you could press certain keys to observe how it affects your pieces’ behaviours. Examples:

- `A` should have all pieces touching the left wall with no rotation.
- `S` should have all pieces 1 space away from the left wall with no rotation.
- `X` should have all pieces 1 space away from the left wall with a CW rotation.
- `;` is on the other hand; all pieces would touch the right wall with no rotation.
- `K` is 2 keys away from `;`, so all pieces would be 2 spaces from the right with no rotation.
- `8` should have all pieces 2 spaces from the right with a 180 rotation.
