# How to Improve at 1-Key-Finesse
Like with guideline Tetris, *you just have to play more.* Trust the process.

A good benchmark to aim for is 1 PPS in a 40L Sprint run (~1:40 time). This shows that you have basic muscle memory for easy placements, and you can take advantage of the consistency of touch typing to improve your accuracy.\
You could also reach 1 PPS easily with the double tap practice mode in Justin1L8’s script. *It’s best to turn that setting off when you’re approaching a sub-1:00 40L Sprint though.*

You’ll also get used to the columnar positions quickly if you force yourself to stack flat. For vertical J/L/T pieces, always follow the 3-step process to ensure you don’t rotate the wrong way ([training tool](https://jstris.jezevec10.com/play/mode/2099) if you use the Tampermonkey script). For pieces rotated 180 degrees, you could try using your stronger and longer fingers to reach the number keys instead of aligning your weaker pinkies first, but ultimately it depends on every player.

The most important piece of advice that I can give is to **never memorize the individual letters for placements!** Although 1KF adopts touch typing technique really well, it is absolutely nothing like typing words / phrases / anything. Just remember the finger movements instead, because usually you’ll type some jumbled up mess like `LAUAFZFDYOAAI3LD9DYACL4K/`.

Now, here’s some advice for different PPS benchmarks from varying skill levels:

## 1 PPS → 1.5 PPS
- Don’t reset your runs, you must learn from all of your mistakes
- Don’t rely on practice modes, stick to the real deal
- Don’t outrun your brain
- Burst flat placements since your fingers are rested on the home row
- Try not to use hold too much

## 1.5 PPS → 2 PPS
- Start getting familiar with vertical L/J/T placements
  - You’ll notice that for all pieces except the I piece, the keys `TY` and `BN` result in the same placement - it doesn’t matter which key you use as long as your accuracy is good
- Look ahead in your queue and plan accordingly, but still focus on accuracy
- Recognize common stacking patterns for quicker thinking, these from [KezDaBez’s TETR.IO guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ctJ57dl3xyg&t=219s) will suffice
  - Heart shapes (LS, LO, JO, JZ)
  - 3x4 boxes
  - Stair patterns for S/Z pieces

## 2 PPS → 3 PPS
By this point you could adopt some sprint techniques from guideline Tetris / other block stacking games:

- To push speed, practice bursting with your queue by placing multiple blocks in quick succession, pausing for a mental break, and repeating this process
- To push consistency, practice playing with a metronome, forcing you to using more pieces in your queue
- Use more patterns if necessary, up to you
- For some old-school 1KF players, you could place I pieces rotated all the way to the walls to reduce pinky motion / usage of weaker fingers

## 3 PPS → 4 PPS
At this point it’ll get a lot harder to improve at 1KF unless you’re already really fast with guideline controls, or if your thinking speed is really fast. Either way, there are still a couple things that you could do:

- "Roll" the easy pattern placements - **this is very situational though**
  - Depending on your stacking preferences your preferred rolls could be way different from mine
- Examples:
  - If you have TSL in your queue with a flat board, roll the keys AER
  - If you have JO in your queue, roll the keys AS or SD or DF etc. to burst a heart shape
  - If you have LOJ in your queue, roll the keys LK and then tap 9 to burst a 3x4 box 1 space away from the right wall
- If you have good habits:
  - Feel free to keep resetting for the ideal run - sometimes not being able to get a good queue can be frustrating
  - Try using your stronger fingers for keys that are harder to reach
Learn sprint openers

## 4 PPS → 5 PPS
I’m not even kidding, just do everything mentioned above but better. Most normal players don’t reach these speeds, but if you made it this far, congratulations!\
5+ PPS will lead you to a sub-20 40L Sprint. Back in 2012, people from Hard Drop were still doubting the feasibility of playing this fast. Now, it’s been more accessible* than ever.\
(*accessible does NOT mean “easy / easier to achieve” in this case)

## 6 PPS, 7 PPS, 8 PPS and beyond
As of writing this, only a single digit number of 1KF players have actually reached 6+ PPS. The biggest setbacks from my experiences are:

- Bad stacking decisions leading to skims (because I’m playing too fast to think of better options)
- Bad queues
- Lack of previews (to a certain extent, it does come down to reaction time)
  - Jstris has 5 previews, Techmino has 6+, and the [Scratch client](https://scratch.mit.edu/projects/1067368439/) can handle up to 8
  - Not being able to burst a full bag of my sprint openers at once can cause some mindblock

## Other Topics

### Stacking Methods
In theory, if the number of possible piece placements goes down (via looping or with pattern stacking), the required thinking time goes down, which leads to faster sprint times. While this is true, usually these methods require holds. Since pressing hold takes 1 key, it takes the same number of keys to hold than to place a whole block on your stack.\
In my opinion, it is faster and better in the long term to do 9-0 (or 0-9) stacking (preferably without holds), as you’ll get to learn all the possible piece placements for other gamemodes outside of sprint. This is what **Justin1L8**, **Wwuly**, **Joseph3079**, **leeyum**, **had0j** and I have done.

Some players have also found success with pattern stacking or looping, limiting the number of necessary keys needed to clear the same objective in Sprint.

### Set-Queue Sprint
Set-queue sprints may not be the best training method to push raw speed, but going as fast as possible with predetermined pieces could help outrun your brain to train your mental further. You can practice this by making your own Jstris custom maps.\
**XaeL** allegedly got a [16 second 40L Sprint](https://harddrop.com/forums/index.php?topic=4379.msg60909#msg60909) with an input sequence of real words, and swng got a [14.05](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1JzQycw-h2-xW_siw0qTzHr7EpTvNJfpl/view) with no-hold ST stacking.

### Alternative Keybinds
As you might have noticed while playing, the `1` key is hard to reach with your left pinky, but you could also use a stronger finger like your left ring finger. That being said, your control scheme doesn’t strictly have to be a 4x10 rectangle from `1` to `/` - instead, you could use other keybinds to reduce finger travel.

- **QED** re-binded `1` to `Caps Lock` for his [3-6 stacking](https://jstris.jezevec10.com/replay/99893105) boxes on the left.
- Some players have also thought of using both `Shift` keys for hold, although that may overload the pinky fingers even more.
